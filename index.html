Redeployment shortlist 40 keepers, synthetic data

Skills are checked against the Northern Occupational Standards Framework (NOSF) using logbook entries and training records. Certificates come from the training record.

How certificate position is defined. It is the hardest missing certificate the posting asks for. Ready now = valid today. Low effort = some modules held, so finish or renew them. Moderate effort = nothing held yet, or a prerequisite such as sea survival must be fixed first. Hard = a long chain, which only HVAP has (MECS stage 3 plus two years of documented switching, or paperwork for an authorisation). Each filter option shows only that position. The levels are my judgement from the scheme rules in the data pack. The pack gives no course lengths or costs, and offshore medical and passport are not in the data, so someone shown as ready or low effort may still have an unknown gap.

Does OSSC make someone more employable? For three of the six jobs it is a hard requirement, so without it a keeper cannot be considered, and SMOC and WAHS both depend on its sea survival module. Only 3 of the 40 keepers hold a valid one. For Marine Electrician and Watch Officer it makes no difference, and the postings do not say that holders are ranked above others who also meet the requirement.

Issuing bodies: HVAP and MECS, Northern Marine Competence Board. OSSC and WAHS, Carrowmore Offshore Training Consortium. SMOC, Cnoc Harbour Trust Training Centre. RCOM, Northern Isles Radio Licensing Office. Marine Electrician and Watch Officer ask for no certificate.

How the numbers work. Skills score is shown as a percentage. Most jobs are matched to the framework occupation with the same title: each topic is scored from how often and how recently the keeper did it. Frequency is the number of separate years with an entry (4 or more years = 1, 2 or 3 years = 0.6, one year = 0.3). Recency is the last year logged (2022 or later = 1, 2012 to 2021 = 0.7, 2002 to 2011 = 0.4, earlier = 0.2). The two are multiplied. A topic is evidenced at 0.6 or more, or with a completed, in-date training module, and partial at 0.2 to 0.59 or with expired or attended-only training. The bands are my judgement, and logs average only about 10 entries a year per keeper, so “separate years” is a rough measure of regularity. Entries dated in the future are counted as 2026. The control above switches to the earlier method, which counts entries only (3 or more = evidenced, 1 or 2 = partial). Score = evidenced statements plus half of the partial ones, divided by all statements. The two survey jobs are scored on the criteria in their postings instead, because their framework topics never appear in lighthouse logs. Certificates are not folded into the score. Each certificate the posting requires gets an effort level worked out from the scheme rules and the keeper’s record: Ready (valid now), Low effort (re-sit or finish modules, prerequisites already met), Moderate (a full set of modules, or a prerequisite to fix first), Hard (a long chain, such as HVAP needing MECS stage 3 and two years of documented switching or paperwork). A job’s certificate position is its hardest missing certificate. Course lengths and costs are not in the data, so the levels are a judgement. Offshore medical and passport are not in the data and are listed as unknown. A log entry counts only when it records something the keeper did themselves. Lines such as stood by, witnessed, read the permit, signed on to a permit, held the keys, or done as instructed are skipped (about 4.5% of all lines), and each topic is matched with short phrase lists written from reading the logs. The phrase lists have not been checked against hand-labelled examples, so treat the scores as indicative and spot-check the cited entries. Date of birth and payroll numbers are not loaded.
